AI & Jobs — Myth or Reality?

Is AI Really Taking Away Jobs?

Artificial Intelligence is changing the way people work. AI can automate repetitive tasks, help employees work faster, and support decision-making Because of this, some jobs and tasks may be reduced or changed. However, AI is not simply replacing all human workers. At the same time, new jobs and opportunities are emerging around AI, data, software, and technology The bigger change is that many jobs are being transformed rather than completely eliminated

Myth or Reality?

❌ Myth: “AI will take everyone's jobs”

AI can replace or automate some tasks, especially repetitive and routine tasks. But most jobs involve multiple tasks that require human skills such as communication, creativity, decision-making, problem-solving, and responsibility

✅ Reality: “AI is changing jobs and the skills people need”

The impact of AI is different for different occupations. Some roles may decrease, some may change, and new roles may emerge. Therefore, the future of work is not simply Humans vs AI. It is increasingly about Humans working with AI.

What Does the WEF Say?

WEF — World Economic Forum

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 highlights major changes expected in the global workforce

According to employer projections in the report, by 2030:

170 million

new jobs could be created

92 million

jobs could be displaced

78 Million

Net growth in jobs is projected by 2030

AI and Machine Learning Specialists and Software and Application Developers are among the technology-related roles identified as some of the fastest-growing occupations

Important: These are projections based on employer expectations, not guaranteed future results

What Does the ILO Say?

ILO — International Labour Organization

The International Labour Organization (ILO) studies how technological changes, including Generative AI, may affect workers and occupations

According to the ILO's 2025 analysis, around 1 in 4 workers worldwide are in occupations with some degree of exposure to Generative AI

However, exposure to AI does not mean that an entire job will automatically disappear. The ILO's analysis indicates that, for most occupations, job transformation is more likely than complete replacement, because many tasks still require human involvement

Key Point AI exposure does not automatically mean job loss. It can also mean that the tasks within a job may change.

Jobs and Opportunities in AI and Technology

The growth of AI is also increasing demand for technology-related skills and occupations

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 identifies several technology-related roles among the fastest-growing jobs by 2030, including:

AI and Machine Learning Specialists

Software and Application Developers

Big Data Specialists

AI Prompt Engineer

The report also identifies technological skills as an important area of growing demand

Examples of AI-related work

AI Engineer: Works on developing and implementing AI systems

Machine Learning Specialist: Works with algorithms and data to develop systems that can learn from data

AI Application Developer: Builds applications that use AI technologies

Data Scientist: Uses data, statistics and computational methods to find useful insights

What Does This Mean for Students?

Students entering the workforce may encounter AI as a part of their everyday professional environment.

Learning technical skills, communication, creativity, critical thinking and problem-solving can help students prepare for changes in the workplace.

AI awareness is not only about understanding AI tools. It is also about understanding how AI may change the way people study, work and build their careers.
